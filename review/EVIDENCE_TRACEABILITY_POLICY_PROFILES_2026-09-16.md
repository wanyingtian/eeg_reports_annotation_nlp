# Evidence traceability as a configurable ladder

**Status:** methodological interpretation and forward design. This names
existing capabilities; it does not alter the completed JBHI results or launch
new inference.

## The policy change in one sentence

Do not choose between “exact only” and “fuzzy/semantic allowed” as if they were
competing definitions of one fact. Compute the full ladder, name the layer used
for each claim, and record the selected claim profile in the run receipt.

## Chris's original method is a building block, not an obsolete era

The public 2025 factuality module uses a three-stage matcher:

1. normalized substring search;
2. fuzzy token matching at 70/100; and
3. whole-report semantic similarity at cosine 0.70.

For positive classifications, a report-category unit is counted as supported
when at least one generated phrase passes any stage. The companion alignment
module asks whether the explanation's inferred positive/negative polarity
agrees with the four-level classification's binary side.

That remains a reproducible **original-thesis compatibility profile**. It is
valuable for continuity and for recovering the question Chris asked. Its
aggregation is permissive by design: one located phrase can qualify a larger
multi-phrase explanation, and semantic similarity can locate a concept without
proving that the generated wording occurs in the report.

The later traceability audit did not override it. It exposed the stages and
their denominators separately so a location result could not silently become a
verbatim-quotation, entailment or clinical-correctness claim.

## The reusable ladder

| Layer | Question answered | Suitable language | Not established |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unchanged quotation | Are the generated bytes present in the report? | “verbatim/unchanged quotation” | Relevance or support |
| Normalized textual equivalence | Does the text match after declared lossless case, whitespace or typography normalization? | “source-equivalent after normalization” | Verbatim identity |
| Source localization | Can fuzzy or semantic retrieval identify a plausible source span? | “located candidate passage” | Entailment or factuality |
| Decision support | Does the passage support rather than contradict the declared category decision? | “decision-supporting evidence” | Clinical correctness |
| Clinical usefulness | Is the evidence relevant, sufficient and useful to a qualified reader? | “clinically reviewed evidence” | Hidden model reasoning or causal faithfulness |

Each layer can use the layers below it without erasing them. A modern system is
therefore allowed to paraphrase, abbreviate or synthesize; it is not forced to
copy source sentences. It earns a broader claim only by passing the appropriate
additional test.

## Named profiles

### Original-thesis compatibility

Run the original three-stage matcher and original any-phrase aggregation on its
declared tabular schema. Use it to reproduce or compare with the original
analysis. Report the result as three-stage source support under that profile,
not as unchanged quotation or clinical factuality.

### Verbatim provenance

Count only unchanged substrings. Retain every other outcome. Use this when the
claim is specifically that a model returned direct source quotations.

### Normalized provenance

Accept unchanged and declared lossless normalization stages. Report unchanged
and normalized counts separately. This is a useful default for operational
systems affected by line wrapping, Unicode punctuation or capitalization.

### Retrieval-assisted review

Use fuzzy and semantic methods to return the best report span, score and method
for human inspection. Optimize this layer for recall, but do not automatically
promote candidates to decision support. This is where modern embedding and
reranking models add the most immediate value.

### Adjudicated decision support

Combine the located span, evidence role, fixed decision and blinded human—or a
separately validated entailment model—judgment. Preserve support,
contradiction, qualification, insufficiency and abstention as distinct states.
This is the appropriate profile for a future explanation-quality comparison.

## What changing the policy gains

- Higher recall for faithful paraphrases, abbreviations and harmless formatting
  changes.
- Freedom to generate concise evidence summaries rather than source copies.
- A clearer prompt-improvement signal: failures can be separated into missing
  source location, wrong evidence role, contradiction and insufficient support.
- Portability across model families and evidence schemas without forcing every
  model into a copying style.
- A path from automated retrieval to qualified review while preserving the
  exact provenance needed for audit.

## What must remain explicit

Every run receipt should name the profile, matcher artifacts, thresholds,
normalization, source unit (whole report, sentence or span), segment aggregation
(`any`, `all` or per-segment), evidence roles and abstention behavior. A project
may declare a default operational profile, but the profile must never be
implicit in a publication result.

The 100-report MedGemma/Mistral result currently supports a configured-system
traceability comparison. Because the systems use different evidence schemas,
model-versus-schema attribution would require the already-proposed 2-by-2
development experiment. The source-first human review is the bridge from
location to decision support; it should proceed before full-cohort generation.
